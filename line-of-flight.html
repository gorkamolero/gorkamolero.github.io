Case · podcast studio and post-production

Line of Flight

Line of Flight is a video podcast I make with a tantra teacher in Missouri. I bring a news story, researched and told with clips and pictures on slides, and my co-host, who hasn't seen any of it, reads it through the occult traditions as it lands. We record remotely on Zencastr, and everything around it, from the studio to the edit, is tooling I built. The first episode was recorded on 14 October 2026 and is in the edit.

Own project · 2026 · Zencastr, Chrome DevTools Protocol, WebRTC, Python, ffmpeg, MediaPipe, Cloudflare

On air

A minute and a quarter of episode 1, as the cut makes it. I open the chapter on gold and my co-host cuts in.

The setup

Two computers and Zencastr. My Mac runs the studio, and my co-host only opens a link. One command brings it all up, and one Record button starts the show's own recording and Zencastr's together.

During the show

  1. dharma start 1 restarts the deck server, writes the show settings to my DJI mic over USB, and opens a Chrome on its own profile with four tabs: the Zencastr room, the presenter with its cockpit, the stage and a camera sender.
  2. The slides are a web app built from my script: markers in the Markdown place each slide, and a sync script turns the draft into the deck.
  3. The Program is the deck drawing the whole show, slides and both faces in their scene, in a hidden Chrome that records its own tab at 3840×2160 in ten-minute parts.
  4. Zencastr is the call, and it records each of us on our own machine, WAV and 1080p video, uploading as we go.
  5. A watcher holds a DevTools Protocol connection to the room tab. It pins Zencastr to my camera and mic, reads its recorder, presses its buttons, and bridges my co-host into the Program through an RTCPeerConnection it injects into Zencastr's page.
  6. My co-host gets one link: the slides through a Cloudflare tunnel with the media from R2, view only, and a start card that opens the Zencastr call in a tab of its own.
  7. The cockpit, inside the presenter, has a line for everything that can fail (each voice in the Program, Zencastr's recording and upload, both cameras, the mic, the headphones, the disk), and a red line blocks Start.
  8. I present from the presenter in Beats mode, each slide as facts to tell rather than a script to read.

After the show

  1. dharma stop waits until my Zencastr tracks show 100% uploaded, then closes everything.
  2. zencastr.py download takes the recording's zip from Zencastr's Library and fetches it again until everyone the Library lists is in it.
  3. dharma cut lays every Program part on Zencastr's timeline by sound, measures each camera's lip sync, puts Zencastr's camera files over the faces and the clean voices under them, and renders a 1280×720 proxy in about 12 minutes.
  4. I review the proxy on a page that plays the session as the edit, jumping over the cuts. The cuts and the camera changes go in two JSON files, anchored on the words of the transcript.
  5. dharma cut go renders the cut in 4K and measures its lip sync, and edl.py --render makes the edit from it in about 40 seconds for a 2 h 22 min episode, re-encoding only the frames at each cut.

The deck

Every slide is HTML on a 1920×1080 stage, built from the script. The deck has two looks, paper and night, and we recorded in night. These four slides from episode 1 are redrawn for this page from the deck's layouts and pictures.

The audio

I wear headphones and a clip-on DJI Mic Mini 2S. Its show settings (mono, low cut, clipping control, gain on the transmitter, noise cancelling) are written to the receiver over USB at every start, through a vendored open-source CLI driven from Python, and the cockpit reads them back once a minute, never while recording. Clipped on, it gave about 14 dB more voice than the MacBook's mic in the same room.

On my side, Zencastr is pinned to that mic by name. The watcher wraps the page's getUserMedia before Zencastr loads, so whatever Zencastr asks for, it gets the named device with echo cancellation, noise suppression and auto gain off. The voice reaches the WAV as the mic gives it.

Every voice is recorded more than once. Zencastr's local WAVs are the masters. The Program carries a mix (my mic live, my co-host over the call, the clips), and a safety file of each voice. My co-host's own mic is also recorded in his slides tab at 64 kbps and streamed to my Mac.

The cut works on Zencastr's timeline. Each ten-minute Program part is placed by cross-correlating loudness envelopes (log RMS at 100 Hz) of my Zencastr voice against my voice in the Program, near where the end of the part before it says it should start, and three one-minute stretches of it have to agree within 50 ms. The first version guessed each part at 598.5 s; the parts are about 1.5 s longer, and by part 9 the picture was 27 seconds off the sound.

The mix is the two clean voices, with the Program's own sound let in only where a clip played, read from the deck's clip log and crossfaded over 150 ms, so no voice is doubled. Where my co-host's Zencastr track has 20 seconds of digital silence, his own-mic recording fills in at his Zencastr level. In episode 1 that was 9.3 minutes.

Loudness is two passes: the whole mix measured with ffmpeg's loudnorm, then one linear gain to −16 LUFS with true peak under −1.5 dB, encoded with Apple's AAC encoder. For a noisy room there's a pass through ElevenLabs' Voice Isolator with an 80 Hz high-pass; in a rain test it took the rain out at every noise-cancelling setting. Mic tests are recorded with sox, because ffmpeg's avfoundation input dropped a 10 ms slice about every tenth of a second on every Mac mic I tried.

Where Zencastr pushed back

Zencastr's local recordings are why the cut sounds and looks the way it does. Running it inside a scripted studio found its edges. Most of these came out of a test rig: a stand-in co-host on my second laptop, driven by script, with his face and voice from a real recording.

  • Stop ends the sessionZencastr's Stop ends the recording, and the next one needs New Recording, which reloads the room and drops the guest to the green room. Pausing doesn't work either: a guest who joins while it's paused is never recorded when it resumes ("timed out waiting for … to report they actually resumed"). So Zencastr records the whole session, and the studio's Stop stops only the Program. The cut finds each take inside the long recording by sound.
  • A dead tab's stretch never finishesIf the guest's call tab dies mid-recording (a reload, a crash), that stretch stays unfinalized on his laptop and the download skips it: 12 of his 15 tracks in one drop test. So his slides tab, a separate tab, records his mic too and streams it in two-second pieces, which wait on his laptop when the network is down. In episode 1 it covered 9.3 minutes.
  • Nothing stops the room closing mid-uploadZencastr's "Leave site?" warning needs a click in the page first (Chrome's rule), and in a scripted studio nobody clicks. dharma stop reads the room's upload state and waits for 100% before it closes anything. A guest who closes his browser early leaves tracks that only that browser can finish, so the checklist has my co-host wait for 100%.
  • Getting the filesThe room's own MP3, WAV and MOV buttons did nothing for me; the Library's zip works. The guest's tracks reach the Library a few minutes after the Stop (a zip 90 seconds after had only mine), and a 2 h 33 min session took about ten minutes a file. The download follows Zencastr's "Downloading file N/M" and fetches again until everyone the Library lists is in the zip.
  • The first camera request sets the sizeIn one Chrome, the first page to open the camera sets its resolution for every page. Zencastr's green room took it at 640×360 and everything followed. The camera sender now opens it first at 1920×1080, every request asks for exactly 16:9 (looser constraints gave square or vertical video), and a check redoes the order if it comes up small.
  • The guest at his tile's sizeZencastr sends a guest at the size of his tile in the room, 640×360, and that's what the live Program gets. I tried stretching his tile to full size and hiding it. It fought Zencastr's own layout, and in a test the call lost him for two and a half minutes, so it went. The live show uses the small picture, and the cut uses his full-size local file.
  • A take cancelled five seconds inOne failed upload request on the guest's side ("Track initialization error") made Zencastr abort a take for everyone, while the studio still showed recording. The watcher now notices when Zencastr isn't recording during a take and presses Record again, and it never starts a New Recording while the guest is in the call.
  • Camera files driftAfter my co-host's Zencastr failed and reloaded 44 minutes into episode 1, his camera file ran 0.11 to 0.14 s behind his voice, and it was black for 8.3 minutes in all. lipsync.py reads each camera every ten minutes (MediaPipe's face mesh, mouth opening against the voice's loudness, cross-correlated over two minutes), the cut takes each frame where that curve says, and a last pass measures the finished cut, where every reading must be within a frame. Where his camera was black, the Program's picture of him stays.
  • File names on a 12-hour clockZencastr's local files are named on a 12-hour UTC clock, so "t12-06am" sorts after "t01-08am". The tools take the folder written last.
