Draft case study · 2022–2023

Maility

Maility was an outbound email SaaS. I co-founded it as CTO and built the product myself. Most of the onboarding ran through a flow in a browser extension I built.

Co-founder / CTO · Campaign orchestration · Mailbox integrations

What I built

A campaign needed to send a sequence over time, choose an available sending account, respect its limits and stop when a prospect replied or unsubscribed. I built the campaign system around scheduled workers, Firestore work records and provider events.

  • RoleCo-founder and CTO, May 2022–June 2023.
  • BackendFirebase Functions, Firestore and Pub/Sub, with Nylas for mail operations.
  • OnboardingA browser-extension flow, with a backend domain-whitelist endpoint.

These diagrams reconstruct the architecture from a recovered backend snapshot and deployed function metadata. The snapshot is later than my time at Maility; the diagrams describe the code recovered, rather than assigning every later revision to me.

The system

Firebase Functions handled client actions, scheduled work and document events. Firestore held campaign execution state, while Nylas supplied mailbox operations and signed callbacks.

From campaign to send

The batch scheduler made prospects ready and created their journeys and first jobs. A client scheduler and client runner selected work; the job runner sent the email and recorded an activity, creating the next step after success.

Scheduling and sending limits

Campaign windows, account budgets and campaign daily caps all influenced which job could run. Accounts were ordered by sent count with randomized ties, campaigns were chosen by weight, and follow-ups stayed with their original sender.

The data behind a campaign

A prospect’s journey tracked sequence progress; jobs described work to do, and activities recorded what happened. Campaign documents kept ownership and scheduling state, while thread identifiers connected activities to mailbox conversations.

Connecting mailboxes and handling replies

The onboarding endpoint recorded a domain whitelist used during Google authorization. Signed Nylas callbacks classified replies and bounces into activities; a separate polling path updated mailbox conversations for eligible incoming messages.

Failure and recovery

Send errors could reschedule a job, mark it failed or invalidate its sending account. The code also had account and job-status guards plus an hourly sending-state recovery worker; the external provider call and the later Firestore commit remained separate operations.

Billing and client ownership

Stripe integration combined managed extension endpoints with custom checkout and subscription code. Plan changes could move sending accounts and rewrite client ownership across campaign records, rather than changing a single role flag.

The deployed function surface

The deployment export contains 98 functions across callable, HTTP, scheduled, Pub/Sub, Firestore and Auth triggers. It also exposes the boundary of the recovery: some deployed implementations are absent, and the managed extensions are separate from the custom backend.
